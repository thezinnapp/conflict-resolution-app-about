The method

Eight steps from tension to common ground.

Each step opens only when you are both ready, so neither person is rushed and the conversation keeps its shape.

  1. 01

    Set the stage

    Find a quiet, private space. Agree on the ground rules and how long each person has the floor.

  2. 02

    The floor

    One person defines the conflict and shares their perspective with “I” statements. The other listens, then reflects back the key points in the speaker’s words. Then you switch.

  3. 03

    Clarifying questions

    Now that you have both been heard, ask each other open, curious questions — understanding first, rebuttals set aside.

  4. 04

    Share needs

    Each person shares what would help them reach common ground: “What I need is…” or “It would help me if…”.

  5. 05

    Brainstorm solutions

    Generate many options together, focused on shared goals. Judging waits until the next step.

  6. 06

    Agree on a solution

    Support the options you are willing to commit to. The ones you both support become your agreement.

  7. 07

    Action plan

    Detail who does what, by when, and how you will both know it is working. Keep goals realistic.

  8. 08

    Follow-up

    Choose a date and time to check in on how it is going, and add a reminder to each of your calendars.

Step 1 · Ground rules

Start with ground rules you both agree to.

Before anyone speaks, the app helps you set the tone. A brief personal check-in confirms you are somewhere private, have set aside enough time, and are here in good faith — to understand, not to win.

  • Eight ground rules — one voice at a time, listen to understand, reflect back in their words, use “I” statements, behaviors rather than personalities, stay calm and work together.
  • Choose your floor time — 2, 3, 5, 7, 10 or 15 minutes each, and who speaks first.
  • Floor courtesy rests the listener’s microphone while the other person speaks, so the speaker can finish uninterrupted.
  • Fair by design — either person can propose a change, and any change asks you both to confirm again.

Step 2 · The floor

Take turns. Truly listen.

Timed turns keep things calm and fair. When you have the floor, you define the conflict and share how you saw it and how you felt. When you are listening, your only job is to understand.

  • A visible timer for each turn, with a gentle note when time is up.
  • Speak your way — talk live, send text, or record a voice message you review before sending.
  • Sentence starters such as “When ___ happened, I felt ___” and “From my side, I experienced…” help you begin.
  • Pause for a breath — either person can start a shared one-minute pause with a guided breathing cue.

Reflect back

Show that you understand.

After each turn, the listener gives a short “snapshot” of the speaker’s key points, feelings and perspective — in the speaker’s words, not their own — and then asks whether they got it right.

  • Heard before answered — reflecting is not a rebuttal, so each person knows they were understood before the conversation moves on.
  • Then questions and needs — open, curious questions come next, followed by what each person needs to reach common ground.
  • Private notes let you jot down key words while you listen. They stay on your device and are not sent.

The Wording Coach

Say it so it can be heard.

Before a message goes out, the Wording Coach reads it on your device and points out phrasing that tends to escalate — blame, labels, demands and all-or-nothing words — then suggests calmer wording as an “I” statement. You choose: send as written, edit the suggestion, or send the suggested wording.

Instead of“You never help with the dishes.”

Try“I would like you to help with the dishes more often.”

Instead of“You made me feel stupid in front of everyone.”

Try“I felt embarrassed when my idea was laughed at during dinner.”

Instead of“You should have called me.”

Try“I would feel more at ease if you called when plans change.”

  • Runs entirely on your device — free, private and instant. Your words are not sent anywhere to be checked.
  • Works for voice, too — with optional transcription, the coach can review a recording before you send it.
  • Gentle guidance on owning the feeling, turning demands into requests, looking forward rather than back, and asking with curiosity.

Solutions · Action plan · Follow-up

Leave with a plan you both own.

Brainstorm freely, then support the options you are willing to commit to. Solutions you both support become your agreement, and the action plan turns it into clear next steps.

  • Idea support — an on-device assistant offers tailored ideas based on the needs you each shared, alongside your own.
  • Who · what · by when — draft actions from your agreed solutions, assign them, set dates and decide how you will measure success.
  • A check-in you both accept — propose a follow-up time, then add it to your own calendar. On iPhone, the app can remind you too.
  • Keep a record — save a summary of your agreement, or print it and save it as a PDF.

Chores and money, plans and expectations, the small frictions that build up between partners, family members, housemates and friends. The app gives those conversations a calm structure, so each person can speak, be understood and move forward together.

We built the app so we do not receive your conversations. There is no user database of messages, recordings, video, notes or plans — they live on your devices.

Educational & self-help

A conversation tool, used with care.

The Conflict Resolution App is an educational, self-help tool for adults. It offers a structured framework, prompts and optional wording suggestions for everyday disagreements. It is not therapy or counseling, not mediation, and not legal advice, and no person or program in the app acts as a mediator or advisor.

Please do not use it for legal matters such as divorce, custody, court-connected cases or disputes with legal deadlines. Agreements made in the app are informal, good-faith commitments between the two of you.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

How do we start a session?

Choose a Live or Standalone session, enter your first name and a neutral, blame-free topic, then share the six-character code with the other person. They join with the code on the iPhone app or at theconflictresolutionapp.com, and you connect as soon as you both have the session open.

Does the person I invite need to pay?

No. Joining a session someone invites you to is free, on the iPhone app or on the web. Only the person who starts sessions needs a membership after the free trial.

Where are our conversations stored?

On your devices. Each person keeps their own copy in the app’s storage, and we do not receive your messages, recordings, video, notes or plans. You can delete a session, or all of your data, in Settings.

Is the live video recorded?

No. Live camera and microphone streams go directly between the two devices and are not recorded by the app. Voice messages are recorded by you, reviewed by you and sent only when you choose.

Do we need to be online at the same time?

Live sessions need you both online together. Standalone sessions let you exchange messages at your own pace, and if you cannot connect, either of you can send an encrypted “sealed update” file through any channel you trust.

Is this therapy, mediation or legal advice?

No. It is an educational, self-help tool that gives two people a structured way to talk. If you need legal, medical, mental-health or financial guidance, please consult a licensed professional.

Which devices does it work on?

Download the iPhone app from the App Store, or use the web app in a modern browser on a phone, tablet or computer. People on different devices can join the same session.

How do I cancel my membership?

On iPhone, use “Manage subscription” in the app or Settings → your name → Subscriptions. On the web, use “Manage billing or cancel” in the app’s Settings, the link in your receipt email, or email us. You keep access until the end of the period you have paid for.

Who can use the app?

Adults 18 and older. Participation is voluntary for both people, and either person may pause or end a session at any time.
